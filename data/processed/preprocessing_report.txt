5. SEQUENCE LENGTH VS BERT'S 512-TOKEN LIMIT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WordPiece produces roughly 1.3 sub-word tokens per word on
email text, so 512 tokens is about 393 whitespace words.

Emails over ~393 words, before : 13,490 (19.3%)
Emails over ~393 words, after  : 13,477 (19.3%)
Longest email after cleaning  : 46,579 words

No email was dropped for being long, and no text was truncated here.
The full cleaned text is preserved so the truncation strategy stays an
open decision for the tokenisation stage. The options are:

  a. Head truncation (truncation=True, max_length=512)
     The default. Cheap, and phishing cues (subject line, greeting,
     urgency, the first link) are usually front-loaded.
  b. Head + tail
     Keep the first ~384 and last ~128 tokens. Catches sign-off cues
     such as spoofed signatures and unsubscribe blocks.
  c. Chunk and pool
     Split long emails into 512-token windows, classify each, pool the
     results. Most faithful, and the most expensive.

With about four fifths of emails already under the limit, option (a) is
a reasonable baseline; (b) is a cheap upgrade worth measuring against it.

6. PREPROCESSING OPERATIONS PERFORMED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Applied, in order:
   1. Mojibake repair on affected rows (ftfy) - 'â€™' becomes an apostrophe
   2. Unicode NFC normalisation (canonical composition)
   3. <script> and <style> blocks removed, including their contents
   4. HTML comments and DOCTYPE declarations removed
   5. HTML tags removed by tag NAME, keeping the text inside them
   6. HTML entities unescaped (&amp; becomes &, &nbsp; becomes a space)
   7. Quoted-printable soft line breaks removed

   9. Base64 blobs of 200+ characters removed (encoded attachments)
  10. Zero-width characters, BOMs and bidi controls removed
  11. Unicode replacement characters removed
  12. C0/C1 control characters removed, keeping tab and newline
  13. Non-breaking and exotic Unicode spaces converted to plain spaces
  14. Runs of spaces and tabs collapsed to one space
  15. Three or more blank lines collapsed to one blank line
  16. Leading and trailing whitespace stripped
  17. Records with no text, or fewer than 5 words, removed
  18. Exact and near duplicates removed after cleaning
  19. Labels validated as exactly {0, 1}

Deliberately NOT applied, because BERT needs the context:
   - no lowercasing            casing is signal, and bert-base-cased may be used
   - no stop-word removal      function words carry the sentence structure
   - no stemming or lemmatising
   - no punctuation stripping  '!!!' and sentence boundaries are signal
   - no URL removal or masking URLs are among the strongest phishing cues
   - no number or currency masking
   - no NFKC folding           it would erase homoglyph attacks on brand names
   - no tokenisation, vectorisation, embedding, splitting or training

Two choices worth flagging:
  * HTML tags are matched by tag name rather than by the generic '<...>'
    shape, so that angle-bracket email addresses such as
    <james@maktoob.com> survive. 553 records in the input contain that
    form, and sender addresses are useful phishing evidence.
  * NFC was chosen over NFKC on purpose. NFKC folds full-width and
    look-alike characters to ASCII, which would destroy exactly the
    homoglyph tricks used to disguise brand names in phishing.
